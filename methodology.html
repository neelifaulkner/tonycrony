Standards & Process

Methodology

This page explains how records are reviewed, how connections are documented, how source material is organized, and how this investigation separates records, analysis, questions, and conclusions.

The goal of this project is not to ask readers to accept claims without evidence. The goal is to organize the records so readers can review the source material and understand how different pieces may connect.

Core Standard

Records First

This investigation is built around public records, official filings, court documents, property records, planning materials, public meeting records, corporate/entity filings, and documented source material whenever available.

01

Documented Facts

A documented fact is information supported by a record, filing, public statement, court document, official database, archived material, interview, or other identifiable source.

02

Analysis

Analysis explains why a record may matter, how timing may overlap, how entities may connect, or why a pattern may raise questions. Analysis should be clearly distinguishable from the underlying document.

03

Open Questions

Some records raise questions that cannot be fully answered from available documents alone. Those questions are identified as questions, not presented as proven conclusions.

04

Connection Does Not Equal Misconduct

A person, business, property, or entity appearing on this site is not automatically accused of wrongdoing. Connections may reflect appearances in records, ownership history, filings, transactions, approvals, litigation, or other documented overlap.

05

Corrections & Context

Corrections, clarifications, additional documentation, and relevant context can be submitted for review. When appropriate, pages may be updated to reflect corrected information or newly obtained records.

Source Review

How Records Are Evaluated

Records are reviewed for dates, parties, signatures, entity names, property descriptions, transaction amounts, approval history, amendments, references to other records, and links to people or entities already appearing elsewhere in the investigation.

When possible, records are compared against other sources, such as property databases, business filings, public meeting materials, court filings, ethics disclosures, public records responses, and archived documents.

This project may also include interviews, tips, or protected-source material. Those materials are handled carefully and are not treated the same as public documents unless they can be corroborated or clearly described as source-based information.

Editorial Standards

How Information Is Presented

Records

Records are presented as source material and should be linked or described whenever possible.

Timeline Entries

Timeline entries are used to establish sequence, timing, and overlap. A timeline entry does not automatically imply wrongdoing.

Network Connections

Network entries are used to help readers follow people, entities, businesses, properties, approvals, transactions, and recurring relationships across records.

Property Threads

Property pages are used to track ownership history, transactions, development activity, approvals, filings, and related records.

Questions

Questions are labeled as questions. They are included when records create reasonable areas for additional review, but they should not be read as final conclusions.

Tips & Corrections

Submit Information

If you have records, corrections, context, or information relevant to this investigation, you can submit it for review. Please include documentation whenever possible.
